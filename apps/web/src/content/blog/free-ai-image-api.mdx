Every "free AI image API" has a catch — a daily request cap, a watermark, a slower queue behind paying traffic, or fine print that blocks commercial use entirely. None of that is hidden maliciously; it's just how a provider funds inference for people who never pay. The honest alternative isn't a bigger free tier — it's a small amount of real, unrestricted credit you spend like any other API call.

## What "free" usually means on a provider's own API

A provider-run free tier exists to get you to a paid plan, so it's shaped to make that conversion likely: a fixed number of requests per day that resets whether you used them or not, output capped below the model's best resolution or quality setting, a visible watermark on generated images, and — easy to miss until legal asks — a terms-of-service clause that free-tier output can't be used commercially. Rate limits are usually tighter too, so a burst of ten requests in a minute queues behind paying accounts instead of running immediately. None of this is a bug; it's the tier doing its job. The catch is just that "free" and "production-ready" aren't the same claim.

## The real trade-off: quota vs. quality vs. speed

Every free tier picks which corner to cut, and it's rarely disclosed up front:

- **Quota caps** — a hard number of images per day or month, often small enough that a real test batch (dozens of prompts, a few variations each) burns the whole thing in one sitting.
- **Quality caps** — the free tier runs a smaller model, a lower resolution ceiling, or disables parameters (seeds, aspect ratios, upscale passes) that the paid tier exposes.
- **Speed caps** — free requests queue behind paid ones, so the same prompt that returns in two seconds on a paid key can sit for a minute or more at peak load.

Comparing "free AI image APIs" without checking which of these three a given provider sacrifices is comparing marketing pages, not capability. The only way to see the real shape of a tier is to pull current limits and pricing straight from the API instead of a docs page that may already be stale:

```bash
curl "https://api.aigenway.com/v1/methods?provider=google_banana" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer sk_live_..."
```

That call returns the live `model` and `method` list for a provider along with current per-unit pricing on your account — the numbers to budget against, not a free-tier FAQ.

## Why $5 of real credit beats a bigger free quota

A bigger daily quota still runs the capped model at the capped resolution with the same watermark — more of a product you can't ship with. $5 of prepaid balance against full pricing is smaller in request count but higher in signal: every image is production quality, so a test batch tells you what the model actually does, not what the free tier allows it to do. And because `/v1/generations` uses one task contract across every provider on the gateway, that same $5 stretches across multiple models — compare Nano Banana against Imagen 4 or gpt-image-1 on the same prompt, at full quality, without opening a second account for each one.

## When a provider's free tier is still the right call

None of this means free tiers are worthless — they're a fine way to confirm an SDK works or sanity-check a prompt before writing real code. The catch only matters once you're building something you intend to ship: watermarked or commercial-restricted output has to be regenerated anyway before launch, so testing against it wastes the one thing a free tier was supposed to save — your time. If the test is "does this prompt produce what I expect," spend it against real pricing from the start and skip the second pass.

## Checking the catch before you commit

Before treating any "free AI image API" as a planning input, read the actual limits rather than the headline:

1. **Confirm the quota window.** Per day, per month, or per account lifetime — resets differently and changes how far a free tier actually gets you.
2. **Check the output, not just the count.** Resolution ceiling, watermark, and disabled parameters tell you whether free-tier output is even comparable to what you'd ship.
3. **Read the commercial-use clause.** Several providers' free tiers are explicitly non-commercial — fine for a prototype, a blocker the moment the project has a customer.
4. **Pull live pricing before committing to a paid tier.** `/v1/methods` on the gateway, or the equivalent on a provider's own dashboard, is the only number that's never stale.

Free is rarely free of trade-offs — it's a quota, a quality cap, or a usage restriction wearing the word "free." $5 of real credit against real pricing answers the question a capped free tier can't: what the model actually costs, and whether its output is something you'd ship.
